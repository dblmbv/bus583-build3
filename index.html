The trap: late 2025 looks like a matcha collapse

What I did not trust, and why

October 2025 shows matcha at exactly 0.0. Every other October ranges 4.8–34.3, while coffee that same month (57.2) looks normal. August 2025 (4.4) is also far below every other August (10.2–48.1). Taken at face value, these two months would suggest matcha interest is falling apart, or inflate 2025's seasonality to a "record." The dataset doesn't say whether 0 means no searches, a value too small to report, or missing data, so I kept the values, flagged them, and checked the conclusion with and without them. The recommendation does not change: the Mar–Jun lift moves only from 2.50x to 2.46x when October 2025 is excluded.

What holds up, and what doesn't

  • Holds: Mar–Jun is matcha's strongest window in every complete year; top-4 months are Mar–Jun in 2019, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025, 3 of 4 in 2020 and 2021, and 2 of 4 in 2018 (an unusually strong February).
  • Holds: No duplicate rows, no repeated months, no gaps from Jan 2018 to Oct 2025.
  • Caution: Six exact zeros in matcha. Five sit in Nov/Dec where nearby values are 2–12, so they are plausible lows; Oct 2025 is not.
  • Caution: July can be strong (58.6 in 2020), so a taper through July is reasonable rather than a hard stop on June 30.
  • Not shown by this data: sales, revenue, margin, region, or who is searching. Search interest is a demand signal, not a purchase.

Decision memo

To: Category Manager, Beverages · From: Dilyara Bulambayeva, Analyst · Date: October 30, 2026 · Tool: Claude (Claude Project "Data to Decision," Python/pandas) · Dataset: BUS583_Week3_CoffeeMatcha_Synthetic (94 months, Jan 2018 – Oct 2025)

Business question. When should a beverage brand prioritize matcha marketing while maintaining its coffee marketing?

Main finding. Matcha search interest is strongly seasonal, and the pattern repeats every year. In all seven complete years (2018–2024), matcha peaked in May or June, and March–June averaged 1.9x to 2.7x the other eight months. Matcha is lowest in November and December. Coffee is steady by comparison (15% month-to-month variation vs 64% for matcha): highest February–May, softest July–October, never far from its average.

Recommendation. Prioritize matcha marketing from March through June, with creative ready in late February and a taper through July. Keep coffee marketing always-on, with its strongest push January–May. Run this as a one-season test checked against sales data before making it a permanent budget rule.

The number I did not trust: October 2025 matcha = 0.0. Every other October ranges from 4.8 to 34.3, while coffee that month (57.2) looks normal. August 2025 (4.4) is also below every other August (10.2–48.1). The dataset does not say whether zero means no searches, a value too small to report, or missing data. Read literally, these values make 2025 look like a record season (3.46x lift) or a collapse. I kept both values, flagged them, and reran without them: the March–June lift moved only from 2.50x to 2.46x, and 2025's lift fell to 2.41x, back within the historical range. The recommendation did not change.

Limits. Search interest is a demand signal, not sales, revenue or profit. Coffee and matcha are each compared only against their own history because the measurement scale is not documented.

Data-trust log

The trap. The last months of 2025 contain matcha values that fit no earlier year: 0.0 in October 2025 and 4.4 in August 2025. 2025 is also incomplete (January–October only). Together they make 2025 look like the strongest matcha season on record (3.46x vs 1.9–2.7x in every complete year) or a sudden collapse. Neither reading holds up.

How I caught it.

  1. Coverage check: counted months per year; 2025 has only 10, so it was kept separate from the seven complete years.
  2. Zero check: listed all six exact zeros in matcha (none in coffee). Five fall in November or December, where other years run 2–12, so they are plausible lows. October 2025 is the only zero outside that season.
  3. Same-month comparison: October 2025 sits below every other October (4.8–34.3) and August 2025 below every other August (10.2–48.1), while coffee looked normal in both months.
  4. Year-by-year split: 2025's 3.46x lift stood out from every complete year, which pointed straight back to those two months.

I also found that both search columns are stored as text, not numbers, in the original file. A spreadsheet formula could skip them silently, so I converted them in code and confirmed every value converted.

What I changed because of it.

  • Built the seasonal pattern on complete years (2018–2024) only and reported 2025 separately with a warning.
  • Did not delete or replace the suspicious values. I showed results as given, then reran without them; the top four months stayed the same in every version.
  • Removed any "matcha is growing" or "matcha is collapsing" claim, and asked that the data owner confirm what a zero means before 2025 is used for trend decisions.
